There is a particular kind of quiet that comes only in the last hour before dawn. The house still ticks, and the wind still moves in the chimney. Yet the mind is quiet, because the day's arguments have not yet begun.

I used to fill that hour. I read, I wrote letters, I made long lists of everything I meant to do. Now, more often, I simply sit. I watch the window turn from black to grey, and from grey to the faintest blue.

What I have learned, if I have learned anything at all, is this: the things that matter most rarely announce themselves. They arrive slowly. They wait. And they ask only that we be still enough to notice.
