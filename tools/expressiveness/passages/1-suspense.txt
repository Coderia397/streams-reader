The lighthouse keeper finished his supper at seven, as he had done every evening for eleven years. Outside, the sea lay flat and silver under a pale sky, and the gulls had settled on the rocks below the lamp room. He wound the clock, trimmed the wick, and sat down by the window with his pipe.

Then the gulls rose, all at once, without a sound. He set the pipe down. Somewhere beneath him, on the iron stairs, a step creaked. Then another. Nobody had crossed the causeway; he would have seen them. The steps were slow and heavy, and they were getting closer.

He reached for the lantern. His hand was shaking. The door handle began, very slowly, to turn.
